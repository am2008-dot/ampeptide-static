# AMP Peptide Quality Assurance

## Introduction

AMP Peptide operates a structured quality assurance system covering every stage of peptide manufacturing, from raw material control to final batch release.

Quality is not treated as a final inspection step. Instead, it is integrated into synthesis, purification, and analytical testing to ensure consistency across different production scales.

Each batch is reviewed using a combination of chromatographic, mass spectrometric, and physicochemical analysis before release.

---

## Quality Philosophy at AMP Peptide

At AMP Peptide, quality assurance is built around three principles:

- Consistency across batches
- Transparent analytical data
- Traceable manufacturing process

Every peptide project is evaluated based on its intended use, whether for research, cosmetic formulation, or further development studies.

We focus on providing reliable analytical results rather than overcomplicated reporting systems.

---

## HPLC Purity Analysis

High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC) is the primary method used to evaluate peptide purity.

AMP Peptide uses analytical HPLC to:

- Determine peptide purity
- Identify impurity profiles
- Compare batch consistency
- Monitor process optimization results

Purity is calculated using area normalization methods under validated chromatographic conditions.

### Typical purity standards:

| Product Type | Purity Level |
| --- | --- |
| Research peptides | ≥95% |
| Standard peptides | ≥98% |
| High-purity peptides | ≥99% |
| GMP-like batches | ≥99.5% |

Each chromatogram is reviewed by the QC team before batch release.

---

## LC-MS Molecular Confirmation

Liquid Chromatography–Mass Spectrometry (LC-MS) is used to confirm peptide identity.

This analysis ensures that the observed molecular weight matches the theoretical sequence.

AMP Peptide uses high-resolution LC-MS systems to:

- Confirm molecular weight accuracy
- Detect truncated or modified impurities
- Verify sequence integrity
- Support batch release decisions

### Acceptance criteria:

- Mass deviation typically within ±1.0 Da for standard peptides
- Clear dominant molecular ion peak required for release

---

## Residual Solvent Control (ICH Q3C)

AMP Peptide follows ICH Q3C guidelines for residual solvent control.

Common solvents monitored include:

- Acetonitrile
- Dimethylformamide (DMF)
- Dichloromethane (DCM)

### Typical limits:

| Solvent | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Acetonitrile | ≤410 ppm |
| DMF | ≤880 ppm |
| DCM | ≤600 ppm |

Gas chromatography (GC) is used for detection and quantification.

All results are documented in batch analytical reports.

---

## Endotoxin Testing (LAL Assay)

For peptides intended for biological or cell-based applications, endotoxin testing is available using the Limulus Amebocyte Lysate (LAL) method.

AMP Peptide provides endotoxin data upon request.

### Typical specification:

- ≤5.0 EU/mg for research-grade peptides
- Stricter limits available for specific applications

Testing is performed under controlled laboratory conditions to ensure reliability.

---

## USP-Based Microbial Control

AMP Peptide applies USP <61> and <62> guidelines for microbial limit testing when required.

This includes:

- Total aerobic microbial count (TAMC)
- Total yeast and mold count (TYMC)
- Pathogen screening where applicable

Typical internal standard:

- ≤100 CFU/g for general peptide products

Microbial testing is not applied uniformly to all products but is available based on customer requirements.

---

## Water Content Control (Karl Fischer Method)

Water content is measured using Karl Fischer titration.

This is important for peptide stability and long-term storage.

### Standard control range:

- ≤5.0% for lyophilized peptides

AMP Peptide uses both coulometric and volumetric methods depending on sample characteristics.

## Certificate of Analysis (COA)

Each released batch includes a Certificate of Analysis (COA) containing:

- Peptide name and sequence
- Batch number
- HPLC purity data
- LC-MS results
- Water content (if applicable)
- Residual solvent data (if applicable)
- Storage conditions

All data are directly linked to production batch records for traceability.

- Sequence characteristics
- Hydrophobicity

- Storage temperature requirements

Typical storage conditions:

- Lyophilized peptides: -20°C recommended
- Short-term handling: 2–8°C under dry conditions
