## Introduction

Cerebrolysin is a standardized, parenterally administered neuropeptide preparation produced by controlled enzymatic digestion of porcine brain proteins. Unlike single-sequence synthetic peptides, Cerebrolysin is a complex mixture of low-molecular-weight peptides (neurotrophic, neuroprotective, and neuroregenerative effects.

Since its clinical introduction in the 1970s, Cerebrolysin has been investigated in over 60 clinical trials and hundreds of preclinical studies, with a cumulative patient exposure exceeding several million treatment courses. For B2B researchers and pharmaceutical developers, Cerebrolysin offers a unique window into the therapeutic potential of multi-target peptide-based neurotrophic intervention.

This review examines Cerebrolysin’s composition, mechanism of action, preclinical evidence, clinical research, safety profile, and considerations for research procurement.

## Composition and Characterization

### What Is in Cerebrolysin?

Cerebrolysin contains approximately 25% low-molecular-weight peptides and 75% free amino acids. The peptide fraction includes fragments — and in some cases functional mimetics — of key neurotrophic factors:

| Component | Functional Relevance |
| --- | --- |
| BDNF-like peptides | Fragments with sequence homology to brain-derived neurotrophic factor; activate TrkB signaling |
| GDNF-like peptides | Glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor mimetics; support dopaminergic neuron survival |
| NT-3-like peptides | Neurotrophin-3 analogues; promote neuronal differentiation and survival |
| CNTF-like peptides | Ciliary neurotrophic factor fragments; support oligodendrocyte survival |
| NGF-like peptides | Nerve growth factor mimetics; enhance cholinergic neuron function |
| Free amino acids | Including glutamate, aspartate, glycine, taurine; provide substrate for neuronal metabolism |

The molecular weight distribution is tightly controlled: approximately 80% of the peptide content is below 5 kDa, 15% between 5–10 kDa, and less than 5% above 10 kDa. This ensures blood-brain barrier (BBB) permeability, which is a critical advantage over full-length recombinant neurotrophic factors.

## Mechanism of Action

Cerebrolysin’s mechanism is best understood as a **multi-target, pleiotropic neurotrophic cascade** rather than a single receptor-ligand interaction.

### 1. Neurotrophin Receptor Activation

Cerebrolysin’s constituent peptides act as partial agonists at neurotrophin receptors:

– **TrkB activation**: BDNF-like peptides bind to TrkB receptors, inducing autophosphorylation and activating the MAPK/ERK and PI3K/Akt pathways. This promotes neuronal survival, synaptic plasticity, and dendritic arborization. – **TrkC activation**: NT-3-like peptides stimulate TrkC receptors, supporting neuronal differentiation and synaptic maturation. – **p75ᴺᵀᴿ modulation**: Some peptide fragments bind the p75 neurotrophin receptor (p75ᴺᵀᴿ), modulating its pro-apoptotic signaling in a context-dependent manner — reducing apoptosis under stress while preserving physiological function.

### 2. Enhancement of Endogenous Neurotrophin Synthesis

Beyond direct receptor activation, Cerebrolysin upregulates the expression of endogenous neurotrophins:

– **BDNF mRNA**: 1.5–3 fold increase in hippocampal and cortical neurons. – **NGF**: Significant upregulation in basal forebrain cholinergic neurons. – **GDNF**: Enhanced expression in striatal astrocytes and microglia.

This dual mechanism — direct receptor activation combined with endogenous synthesis upregulation — produces sustained neurotrophic support that persists beyond the drug’s pharmacokinetic half-life.

### 3. Amyloid-Beta Clearance

In Alzheimer’s disease models, Cerebrolysin has been shown to:

– Reduce Aβ₁₋₄₂ aggregation and deposition. – Enhance microglial phagocytosis of Aβ oligomers. – Upregulate neprilysin and insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE), key Aβ-degrading proteases. – Reduce tau hyperphosphorylation through GSK-3β inhibition.

### 4. Synaptic Plasticity and Neuronal Excitability

Electrophysiological studies demonstrate that Cerebrolysin:

– Enhances long-term potentiation (LTP) at hippocampal CA3-CA1 synapses. – Increases dendritic spine density in cortical pyramidal neurons. – Modulates NMDA receptor subunit composition (increasing GluN2A:GluN2B ratio), favoring synaptic over extrasynaptic signaling.

### 5. Neuroinflammation and Oxidative Stress

Cerebrolysin attenuates neuroinflammatory pathways:

– **Microglial modulation**: Shifts M1 (pro-inflammatory) to M2 (anti-inflammatory) phenotype. – **Cytokine regulation**: Reduces TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6; increases IL-10 and TGF-β. – **Oxidative stress**: Decreases malondialdehyde (MDA) and 4-hydroxynonenal (4-HNE); increases superoxide dismutase (SOD) and glutathione peroxidase (GPx) activity. – **Mitochondrial protection**: Preserves mitochondrial membrane potential and ATP production under oxidative challenge.

## Conclusion

Cerebrolysin remains one of the most extensively characterized neuropeptide preparations in scientific literature, with a mechanism that combines direct neurotrophin receptor activation, enhancement of endogenous neurotrophic support, modulation of amyloid pathology, and attenuation of neuroinflammation. Its multi-target pharmacology — enabled by its controlled peptide composition and BBB permeability — positions it as a unique research tool for investigating neurotrophic mechanisms in aging, neurodegeneration, and brain injury.
