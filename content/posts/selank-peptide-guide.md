## Introduction

Selank is a synthetic heptapeptide (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro) originally developed at the Institute of Molecular Genetics of the Russian Academy of Sciences. Structurally derived from the human immunoglobulin G (IgG) heavy chain, Selank belongs to the class of **regulatory peptide-based nootropics and anxiolytics**. Unlike classical benzodiazepines, which exert their effects through GABA⁻A receptor modulation, Selank operates through a multi-target mechanism involving neurotrophin signaling, monoaminergic modulation, and immunoregulatory pathways.

This article provides a comprehensive scientific overview of Selank for B2B buyers, research institutions, and drug development professionals — covering its structural characteristics, mechanism of action, preclinical and clinical evidence, safety profile, and research applications.

## Structural Characteristics

Selank is a linear heptapeptide with the following properties:

| Property | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Sequence | Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro (TKPRPGP) |
| Molecular Formula | C₃₃H₅₇N₁₁O₉ |
| Molecular Weight | ~751.9 Da |
| Synthesis | Solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS) with Fmoc/t-Bu strategy |
| Solubility | Highly soluble in water (>50 mg/mL) |
| Stability | Stable in lyophilized form at -20°C; solutions should be used within 24–48 hours at 4°C |

The presence of proline residues at positions 3, 6, and 7 imparts conformational rigidity, which contributes to Selank’s resistance to rapid proteolytic degradation compared to linear peptides of similar length. The N-terminal threonine and lysine residues are critical for receptor recognition and downstream signaling.

## Mechanism of Action

Selank’s pharmacological profile is distinguished by its **multi-modal mechanism**, which involves at least three parallel signaling axes:

### 1. TGF-β Signaling Pathway

One of the most significant discoveries regarding Selank’s mechanism is its modulation of the **transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) signaling pathway**. Research by Zolotarev et al. (2013, 2017) demonstrated that:

– Selank binds to the type II TGF-β receptor (TβRII) with moderate affinity (Kd ~10⁻⁶ M). – Binding induces phosphorylation of Smad2/3 transcription factors, leading to downstream gene expression changes. – TGF-β pathway activation upregulates expression of **brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF)**, neurotrophin-3 (NT-3), and glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF).

This TGF-β → Smad → BDNF signaling cascade is believed to be a central mechanism through which Selank exerts both its anxiolytic and neuroprotective effects.

### 2. BDNF and Neurotrophin Modulation

Selank has been shown to increase BDNF mRNA and protein levels in the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex — brain regions critically involved in anxiety regulation and cognitive function. The BDNF-TrkB signaling axis mediates:

– **Synaptic plasticity**: Enhanced long-term potentiation (LTP) in hippocampal CA1 neurons. – **Neurogenesis**: Increased survival and differentiation of neural progenitor cells in the dentate gyrus. – **Stress resilience**: Upregulation of BDNF counteracts stress-induced dendritic atrophy in the medial prefrontal cortex.

### 3. Monoaminergic System Modulation

Unlike SSRIs and benzodiazepines, Selank does not directly bind to serotonin or GABA receptors. Instead, it modulates the **metabolism and turnover** of key monoamines:

– **Serotonin (5-HT)**: Increased 5-HT turnover in the frontal cortex and striatum, without altering baseline receptor occupancy. – **Dopamine**: Modest elevation of dopamine levels in the nucleus accumbens, correlated with improved motivation and reward processing in animal models. – **Norepinephrine**: Normalization of stress-induced norepinephrine elevation in the locus coeruleus.

This indirect, modulatory approach produces anxiolysis without the sedative, amnestic, or dependence liabilities associated with benzodiazepines.

### 4. Immunomodulatory Effects

Selank also exhibits significant immunomodulatory activity, consistent with its IgG-derived origin. It:

– Suppresses pro-inflammatory cytokine release (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6) in LPS-stimulated microglia. – Enhances IL-10 production, promoting an anti-inflammatory M2 microglial phenotype. – Reduces oxidative stress markers (MDA, ROS) in neuronal cultures exposed to glutamate excitotoxicity.

These immunomodulatory properties are increasingly recognized as contributing to Selank’s neuroprotective profile, particularly in models of chronic stress and neurodegeneration.

## Preclinical Evidence

### Rodent Anxiety Models

Selank has been extensively evaluated in validated rodent models of anxiety:

| Model | Dose Range | Effect | Reference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Elevated Plus Maze (EPM) | 0.1–1.0 mg/kg i.p. | Increased open-arm entries and time — comparable to diazepam at 1.0 mg/kg, without motor impairment | Seredenin et al. (2005) |
| Light-Dark Box Test | 0.1–0.5 mg/kg i.p. | Increased time in illuminated compartment | Kozlovskaya et al. (2008) |
| Social Interaction Test | 0.25–1.0 mg/kg i.p. | Increased social interaction time without agonist-induced aggression | Kolomin et al. (2014) |
| Vogel Conflict Test | 0.5 mg/kg i.p. | Increased punished drinking (anti-conflict effect) | Andreeva et al. (2010) |

Critically, Selank did not produce significant effects on rotarod performance or open-field locomotor activity — indicating a **clean dissociation between anxiolysis and sedation**.

### Cognitive Enhancement Studies

In rodent models of cognitive impairment (scopolamine-induced amnesia, chronic stress), Selank (0.1–0.5 mg/kg) improved performance in:

– Morris water maze: Reduced escape latency and increased platform crossings. – Novel object recognition: Increased discrimination index. – Passive avoidance: Improved retention latency.

These effects were correlated with increased hippocampal BDNF levels and enhanced LTP in CA1 synapses.

## Human Clinical Evidence

Selank has been investigated in several Phase I and Phase II clinical trials in Russia:

### Phase I — Safety and Tolerability

A double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase I study (n = 40 healthy volunteers) evaluated single and repeated doses of Selank (intranasal administration, 100–600 μg/day for 14 days). Results:

– No serious adverse events reported. – No clinically significant changes in vital signs, ECG, or laboratory parameters. – No evidence of sedation, cognitive impairment, or withdrawal symptoms upon discontinuation. – Maximum tolerated dose was not reached at 600 μg/day.

### Phase II — Generalized Anxiety Disorder

In a Phase II trial involving 80 patients with Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD), Selank (intranasal 400 μg/day for 21 days) was compared to placebo:

| Outcome | Selank | Placebo | p-value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reduction in HAM-A score | −14.2 ± 3.1 | −6.8 ± 2.9 |  |
| Clinical Global Impression (CGI-I) responders | 71% | 32% |  |
| Onset of anxiolytic effect | Day 3–5 | Day 10–14 | — |
| Sedation / somnolence | 5% | 8% | n.s. |

The rapid onset of action (3–5 days vs. 2–3 weeks for SSRIs) represents a potential therapeutic advantage.

## Safety Profile and Toxicology

### Acute Toxicity

– LD₅₀ in rodents: >5,000 mg/kg p.o. (classified as practically non-toxic). – No mortality at doses up to 100 mg/kg i.p.

### Subchronic Toxicity

– 90-day repeated-dose study in rats (0.5, 2.5, and 10 mg/kg/day i.p.) – No target organ toxicity. – No significant hematological, biochemical, or histopathological findings.

### Genotoxicity

– Negative in Ames test, chromosomal aberration assay, and micronucleus test.

### Abuse Potential

– No conditioned place preference (CPP) at anxiolytic doses, suggesting low abuse liability. – No withdrawal syndrome upon abrupt discontinuation after 21-day treatment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1: Is Selank FDA-approved for clinical use?**  
 No. Selank is not FDA-approved for clinical use in the United States. It is approved as a pharmaceutical product in Russia (since 2009) but is available for research purposes only in most other countries.  
 **Q2: What is the mechanism of action of Selank?**  
 Selank primarily acts through modulation of TGF-beta signaling pathways, leading to upregulation of BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor). It also modulates monoamine neurotransmitter systems (serotonin, dopamine) and has immunoregulatory activity through cytokine modulation.  
 **Q3: What purity grade is recommended for Selank research?**  
 For in vitro studies, 95% purity may be sufficient. For in vivo research and behavioral studies, 98% or higher purity is strongly recommended to minimize confounding effects from truncated or deletion-related impurities.  
 **Q4: How is Selank typically administered in animal models?**  
 Selank is most commonly administered intranasally (for CNS targeting) or via subcutaneous injection in rodent models. Doses typically range from 100 to 500 microgram/kg depending on the study design.  
 **Q5: What is the stability profile of lyophilized Selank?**  
 Lyophilized Selank is stable for 24+ months when stored at -20 degrees C, desiccated, and protected from light. In solution, it is stable for approximately 7 days at 4 degrees C. Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles.
